The creative routes we'd test first

Run these within your own program. Each route has a different job and a clear purchase destination.

Product demonstration

Make the first use easy to picture

Use a creator who can film a clear close-up and describe their own experience. Show the TPU head, replacement mechanism and complete brushing setup. Link to the brush page.

Proposed brief: “Show what arrived, how you change the head, and what using the brush feels like. Keep the description in your own words.”

Compare: new-customer order rate from this route against a routine-led post with the same offer and attribution rules.

Who to recruit and how to structure the offer

Grooming and routine creators

Look for recent, clear demonstrations of products used at home, an audience concentrated in the chosen market, and comments asking practical product questions. Check audience analytics and conflicting partnerships directly before selecting anyone.

Dental educators

Explore qualified professionals who can explain product use accurately and are comfortable assessing the brief. Their participation depends on independent product review and agreement on supported claims.

Consider product gifting plus commission where a creator accepts those terms, or a fixed production fee plus commission for agreed deliverables. Negotiate usage rights separately. Set the commission and any fee from your actual margin allowance; this scan quotes no rates.

A proposed first month

Choose one priority European market with stock, shipping and a matching storefront confirmed. Build the pilot into the creator and affiliate setup you are developing.

Week 1

Agree the market and offer. Check creator audience data and conflicts. Confirm usage rights, disclosure and the commission rules. Test links, codes, deduplication and commission reporting with an end-to-end order.

Week 2

As a starting proposal, have two creators each publish a demonstration and a routine post. Give every post its own tracking link. Use the same brush offer and separate posting dates.

Week 3

Read orders alongside clicks and product-page conversion. Rework the specific step that loses buyers. Reuse creator footage only within agreed rights.

Week 4

Make a provisional read of costs and first orders. Finish the contribution read after each order's 30-day return window. Track repeat purchases beyond this month.

This small pilot gives directional observations; creator, timing and audience differences limit comparisons. Define order rate as attributed new-customer orders divided by tracked visits. Repeat a test when completed orders support positive contribution after all allocated costs within your allowance. Wait for more data when the read is too uncertain.

Measure the economics of each creator

Attribute orders consistently

Use unique links and codes, one agreed attribution window, and order-level deduplication. Record existing customers separately. A code redemption alone cannot establish an incremental sale.

Include the complete test cost

Include creator fees, samples and shipping, commission, production and any paid distribution or management cost allocated to the test.

Keep repeat purchases visible

Follow head and toothpaste reorders over the observed cohort period. The first month cannot establish lifetime value.

Set the allowable cost from your own margin

First-order revenue after discounts and tax, before refunds
minus product and fulfilment costs
minus payment costs and expected refund losses
minus your required contribution
equals the acquisition allowance per new customer.

Use a first-order allowance for the first-order customer acquisition cost comparison. Count refunds and other costs once. Include commission in acquisition costs. FRESH 32's actual margins and allowance have not been supplied.

Report cost per attributed new customer alongside the uncertainty about incremental sales. Scale decisions should use sufficient completed orders and a consistent return window.
