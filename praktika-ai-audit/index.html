How this page was built
  • Everything on this page came from the YouTube Data API v3 (search.list, videos.list, channels.list), over a window of 5 March 2026 to 1 September 2026 and read on 2 September 2026. No scraping, no paid tool, no logged-in session, no third-party estimate.
  • The sample is four category searches, page one of each. A search returns at most 50 videos, so every count here is a floor, never a market size: 80 distinct videos across 47 channels, published in the 180 days to 1 September 2026. The four category searches returned 162 videos inside the window, 95 of them long-form. 15 of them are excluded from the sample: 3 did not pass the market-language test, because a search language preference is a bias and not a filter, 11 carry a title from the neighbouring category this page holds out by rule, or sit on a channel that mostly publishes it and 1 sits on a channel we read whose own tagged uploads are all in another language, which the roster and the chart reject for the same reason. Two sampled titles are withheld from display: the creator’s wording carries a phrase this page’s copy rules forbid, and a title is never edited. A further Two titles behind the cards and the picture slots are withheld on the same rule, and are counted everywhere they belong. YouTube’s own total-results figure is never printed.
  • A placement is a window video, long-form and five minutes or longer, where a researched brand won a disclosure phrase within 250 characters of its own name. Every competitor count on this page carries that five-minute floor, and the 03 map counts your own deals without it, so a shorter sponsored video of yours is named rather than dropped. The platform paid label on its own attributes to nobody, and a phrase that names its party inline is spent on that party. A firm placement carries the paid label or a sponsor sentence; a creator-code-only disclosure is counted separately as a code deal. A brand named with no attributable disclosure is a mention, never a placement: 118 mentions were discarded on that rule.
  • Before any of that, each channel’s standing description text is blanked out: any line or 80-character run appearing in at least half of its stored descriptions, once it has five of them. An evergreen affiliate link is standing text, not a deal, so a brand that survives only inside it is recorded and never counted: 0 window videos landed there.
  • We read each channel’s 50 most recent uploads, so every per-channel count is a floor as well. A channel’s median is the views of the last 10 long-form uploads it published inside the window, where long-form means at least 300 seconds and not a livestream, and on an even count the median is the mean of the two middle values. Fewer than 5 leaves no median and no tier: 39 read channels sit there, unmeasured rather than under the floor. Shorts under 60 seconds are counted once, separately, and sit in no median: 28 in the window sample. A channel publishing more Shorts than long-form inside the window is held out of the roster; eight of them still plot on the chart, marked held out. A roster row needs a median of at least 3,000 and a long-form upload inside 90 days, an owner path we can point at (a channel description, a link or an email in the descriptions we read, or a custom handle), at least half of those ten window titles naming this category, and no more than half of them naming the neighbouring category this page holds out by rule. The sample obeys those same category floors, video by video.
  • English-language channels means any channel that clears the language floor alone, because no receipt this page rests on evidences a country: at least 70 per cent of the window uploads that carry an audio-language field are in English, over at least three of them, or, below three, at least 70 per cent of the window titles classify as English on a fixed stopword list. If a channel tags an audio language at all and not one of those tags is English, it fails outright and the title test is never reached. The market is the receipted market: the product is sold and operated in English to English-speaking learners, with no country gate (English store copy on Google Play and the App Store, plus the Series A coverage of a product sold across a hundred countries, read 1 September 2026). English-language channels; no country receipt exists, so no country gate is applied. The market rule is the language floor, and it runs twice. The roster and the chart are held to it channel by channel; the sample is held to it video by video, on each video's own audio tag or title. This page applies no country gate. 1 video was dropped from the sample because the channel they sit on fails that floor. The placement counts are read across every channel the searches surfaced, in any country and any language, because a competitor’s spend is evidence wherever it sits.
