01 · THE ANATOMY

What a creator quote is made of

A creator fee is the number everyone looks at. Four other lines decide what that number buys. Most proposals mention some of them, and leave the rest for you to ask about.

1 · Creator fee

What the creator charges for making and posting the content on their own channel. One dedicated video, one integration, one short. It is the line every proposal shows.

Ask: exactly which uploads are included, on which channel, and how long the mention runs inside the video.

2 · Paid-use rights

Permission to run the creator's video as your own ad, on your own accounts. Also called usage rights or whitelisting. Without it, the video lives on their channel only and you cannot put budget behind it.

Ask: is it included or extra, for how many days, on which platforms, and in which countries.

3 · Edited versions

Extra cuts made from the same shoot. A 15-second vertical for TikTok, a version with a different opening line, a cut without the creator's intro. Some creators include a few, some charge per version, some do not allow edits at all.

Ask: how many versions, who edits them, and whether the creator approves the final cut.

4 · Other costs

Agency or platform fee, production support, tracking links, gifting or product seeding, paid boosts. Small on their own, easy to lose between the proposal and the invoice.

Ask: for the full invoice total, and whether tax is inside or on top.

5 · Terms

Usage term (30 days, 90 days, a year), platforms and territories covered, exclusivity against similar games or apps, release dates and approval deadlines, what gets reported and when. These lines rarely have a price next to them. They still change what you are buying.

Ask: for each term in writing before comparing two totals.

02 · THE WORKED EXAMPLE

One example, line by line

Two hypothetical proposals for one YouTube upload. Booking A quotes €4,000. Booking B quotes €6,000. Read only the first line and A looks cheaper by a third.

What changed

Nothing about the creators. Only the scope. Once rights and edits are on both sides, A costs €250 more than B for the same list of deliverables.

What did not change

Neither proposal says what share of the audience is in your markets, what backs the view estimate, whether the creator is exclusive against similar games, or which outcome will be reported and when. Those are the questions the worksheet prints for you.

What the worksheet would not do

It will not tell you A or B. Two equal totals can still be two very different creators. The decision stays yours, with the numbers now on the same footing.

03 · HOW TO USE IT

Three steps, ten minutes

Have the proposals open next to the worksheet. Copy what they say. Leave blank what they do not say. Blank is useful: it becomes a question.

1 Copy the fees

Name each booking. Enter the quoted fee and any other charge the proposal lists. If a number is not there, leave the field empty. Do not guess.

2 Mark rights and edits

For each booking, say whether paid-use rights and edited versions are included, an extra charge, not needed, or unconfirmed. An extra charge asks for its amount. Unconfirmed becomes a question.

3 Read, then send the questions

Each booking gets a card: the known total, whether it is complete, and the questions still open. Copy the questions into your reply to the supplier. Save the page as a PDF for whoever signs off the budget.

05 · BEFORE YOU SIGN

Questions to send back

The full list the worksheet draws from. Send the ones your proposals leave open. A supplier who answers all of them in one reply is usually a supplier worth booking.

Costs

  • Is the quoted fee the full invoice total, and is tax inside or on top?
  • Which uploads and content pieces does the fee include?
  • Are there agency, platform, production or tracking charges beyond the fee?

Rights and edits

  • Are paid-use rights included, and if not, what do they cost?
  • For how many days, on which platforms and in which territories can we run the video as an ad?
  • How many edited versions are included, who edits them, and does the creator approve the final cut?

Audience

  • What share of the audience is in our target markets, and what supports that figure?
  • What supports the view estimate: recent uploads, a screenshot, a platform report?
  • What exclusivity is included against similar games or apps, and for how long?

Timing and reporting

  • When will the draft, our feedback and the final upload happen?
  • Which outcome will be reported, under what attribution rules, and on what date?
  • What happens if the upload slips past the agreed date?

06 · GLOSSARY

The words on the proposal

Plain definitions for the terms that move the price. Suppliers use several names for the same thing; these are the ones you will meet most.

Creator fee
What the creator charges to make and post the content on their own channel. Sometimes called the talent fee or booking fee.
Dedicated video vs integration
A dedicated video is about your game or app from start to finish. An integration is a segment inside a video about something else. Same creator, very different fee and attention.
Paid-use rights
Permission to run the creator's content as your own paid ad. Also called usage rights, ad rights or whitelisting. Priced by term, platform and territory.
Usage term
How long the paid-use rights last, counted from the upload or from the first ad. 30, 60, 90 days and 12 months are common.
Territories
The countries where you may run the content as an ad. Worldwide costs more than one market.
Edited versions
Extra cuts from the same footage: vertical versions, shorter versions, versions with a different opening. Also called cutdowns or derivatives.
Exclusivity
The creator agrees not to promote competing games or apps for a period. Category exclusivity is wider than named-competitor exclusivity, and priced accordingly.
Deliverables
The list of things the creator will produce and post: one long-form video, two shorts, one story, and so on. The fee should map to this list exactly.
CPI
Cost per install: spend divided by installs attributed to the campaign. Your target CPI turns a budget into the number of installs it has to bring.
Attribution window
How long after seeing the content an install or purchase still counts for the campaign. Agree it before the campaign, or two reports will disagree afterwards.
Approval rounds
How many times you can review the draft before it goes live, and by when. One round is common. Zero rounds is a risk worth a question.
Seeding
Sending product, keys or credit to the creator so they can show the game or app. A cost on your side even when the creator fee is zero.
